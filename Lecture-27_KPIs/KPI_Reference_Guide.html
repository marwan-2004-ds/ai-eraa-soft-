1. Introduction and Conventions

A Key Performance Indicator (KPI) is a quantifiable measure that shows how effectively an organization is achieving a specific business objective. This guide collects 40 widely used KPIs across nine domains and documents each one in the same structure, so teams use one shared definition for every number they report.

Each KPI includes: name, definition, formula, purpose, unit of measurement, data requirements, a worked numerical example with interpretation, and a source.

Conventions used throughout:

  • Reporting period: unless stated otherwise, every KPI is computed over one period (month, quarter or year); numerator and denominator always cover the same period.
  • Percentages are calculated as (numerator ÷ denominator) × 100.
  • Currency is shown in US dollars ($) for examples; any currency can be used consistently.
  • "Average" headcount/customers = (start of period + end of period) ÷ 2.
  • Example figures are illustrative, not industry benchmarks.

2. Marketing

2.1 Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)

DefinitionAverage cost to acquire one new paying customer.
FormulaCAC = (Total Sales + Marketing Spend in period) ÷ New Customers Acquired in period
PurposeShows the efficiency of acquisition spending; compared with CLV to judge whether growth is profitable.
UnitCurrency per customer
Data RequirementsMarketing and sales costs (ads, salaries, tools); count of new customers.
Example$50,000 spend ÷ 250 new customers = $200 CAC. Each new customer costs $200 to win.
SourceHubSpot, "Customer Acquisition Cost"; Harvard Business School Online.
Variation: some companies calculate a "marketing-only CAC" excluding sales salaries. State which version is used.

2.2 Return on Ad Spend (ROAS)

DefinitionRevenue generated for every unit of currency spent on advertising.
FormulaROAS = Revenue Attributed to Ads ÷ Ad Spend
PurposeEvaluates campaign and channel effectiveness to decide where to put budget.
UnitRatio (e.g. 4:1 or 4×)
Data RequirementsAd spend per campaign; attributed revenue (attribution model).
Example$40,000 revenue ÷ $10,000 spend = 4.0×. Each $1 of ads returned $4 in revenue.
SourceGoogle Ads Help, "Target ROAS bidding".

2.3 Conversion Rate

DefinitionShare of visitors or leads who complete a desired action (purchase, sign-up).
FormulaConversion Rate = (Conversions ÷ Total Visitors) × 100
PurposeMeasures how well a page, funnel or campaign turns traffic into results.
UnitPercentage
Data RequirementsSessions/visitors; conversion events (web analytics).
Example300 purchases ÷ 12,000 visitors × 100 = 2.5%.
SourceGoogle Analytics Help, "Conversion rate".

2.4 Click-Through Rate (CTR)

DefinitionShare of people who click an ad, email link or search result after seeing it.
FormulaCTR = (Clicks ÷ Impressions) × 100
PurposeIndicates relevance and appeal of creative, copy and targeting.
UnitPercentage
Data RequirementsImpressions and clicks from the ad or email platform.
Example1,800 clicks ÷ 90,000 impressions × 100 = 2.0%.
SourceGoogle Ads Help, "Clickthrough rate (CTR): Definition".

2.5 Marketing Qualified Lead (MQL) to SQL Rate

DefinitionShare of marketing-qualified leads that sales accepts as sales-qualified.
FormulaMQL→SQL Rate = (SQLs from period's MQLs ÷ MQLs) × 100
PurposeMeasures lead quality and alignment between marketing and sales.
UnitPercentage
Data RequirementsCRM lead stages and timestamps.
Example120 SQLs ÷ 400 MQLs × 100 = 30%.
SourceSalesforce, "What Is a Marketing Qualified Lead?".

3. Sales

3.1 Win Rate

DefinitionShare of closed opportunities that were won.
FormulaWin Rate = (Closed-Won Deals ÷ (Closed-Won + Closed-Lost Deals)) × 100
PurposeMeasures sales effectiveness and qualification quality.
UnitPercentage
Data RequirementsCRM opportunities with final status in the period.
Example45 won ÷ (45 + 105) × 100 = 30%.
SourceSalesforce, "Sales Metrics".

3.2 Average Deal Size

DefinitionMean revenue value of won deals.
FormulaAverage Deal Size = Total Revenue from Won Deals ÷ Number of Won Deals
PurposeUsed for forecasting and to track up-market or down-market movement.
UnitCurrency
Data RequirementsWon deal values and counts.
Example$900,000 ÷ 45 deals = $20,000.
SourceHubSpot, "Sales Metrics to Track".

3.3 Sales Cycle Length

DefinitionAverage time from opportunity creation to deal close (won).
FormulaSales Cycle = Σ (Close Date − Creation Date) for won deals ÷ Number of Won Deals
PurposeShows pipeline velocity and helps cash-flow forecasting.
UnitDays
Data RequirementsOpportunity creation and close dates.
ExampleTotal 2,700 days across 45 deals = 60 days.
SourceSalesforce, "Sales Cycle".

3.4 Quota Attainment

DefinitionActual sales achieved as a share of the assigned target.
FormulaQuota Attainment = (Actual Sales ÷ Sales Quota) × 100
PurposeEvaluates individual and team performance; checks whether quotas are realistic.
UnitPercentage
Data RequirementsQuota per rep/team; booked revenue.
Example$270,000 ÷ $300,000 × 100 = 90%.
SourceGartner, "Sales Performance Management".

4. Product

4.1 Daily / Monthly Active Users (DAU / MAU)

DefinitionNumber of unique users who perform a meaningful action in a day (DAU) or 30-day window (MAU).
FormulaMAU = COUNT(DISTINCT user_id with qualifying activity in last 30 days)
PurposeCore measure of product reach and engagement.
UnitCount of users
Data RequirementsEvent logs with user IDs and timestamps; definition of "active".
Example48,000 distinct users active in October = MAU 48,000.
SourceAmplitude, "Active Users"; Mixpanel docs.

4.2 Stickiness (DAU/MAU Ratio)

DefinitionShare of monthly users who use the product on a typical day.
FormulaStickiness = (Average DAU ÷ MAU) × 100
PurposeShows habit formation and how often users return.
UnitPercentage
Data RequirementsDAU averaged over the month; MAU.
Example12,000 ÷ 48,000 × 100 = 25% — the average user is active about 7.5 days per month.
SourceMixpanel, "DAU/MAU ratio".

4.3 Feature Adoption Rate

DefinitionShare of active users who used a specific feature.
FormulaFeature Adoption = (Users Who Used Feature ÷ Total Active Users) × 100
PurposeValidates whether new features deliver value and guides the roadmap.
UnitPercentage
Data RequirementsFeature usage events; active user count.
Example9,600 ÷ 48,000 × 100 = 20%.
SourcePendo, "Feature Adoption".

4.4 User Retention Rate (Day-N)

DefinitionShare of a sign-up cohort still active N days later.
FormulaDay-N Retention = (Cohort Users Active on Day N ÷ Cohort Size) × 100
PurposeShows whether the product delivers lasting value (product-market fit).
UnitPercentage
Data RequirementsSign-up dates; activity events per user.
ExampleOf 2,000 users who joined on 1 Oct, 700 were active on day 30: 35%.
SourceAmplitude, "Retention Analysis".

5. Customer Success

5.1 Customer Churn Rate

DefinitionShare of customers lost during a period.
FormulaChurn Rate = (Customers Lost in Period ÷ Customers at Start of Period) × 100
PurposeMeasures retention problems; high churn erodes growth.
UnitPercentage
Data RequirementsCustomer count at period start; cancellations (excluding new customers who joined and left in-period, or stated otherwise).
Example60 lost ÷ 2,000 at start × 100 = 3% monthly churn.
SourceHubSpot, "Customer Churn Rate".

5.2 Net Revenue Retention (NRR)

DefinitionRevenue kept from existing customers including expansion, minus contraction and churn.
FormulaNRR = ((Starting MRR + Expansion − Contraction − Churned MRR) ÷ Starting MRR) × 100
PurposeShows whether the existing base grows on its own; >100% means growth without new customers.
UnitPercentage
Data RequirementsMRR per customer at start and end; upgrade/downgrade/cancel events.
Example(100,000 + 15,000 − 4,000 − 6,000) ÷ 100,000 × 100 = 105%.
SourceStripe, "Net revenue retention"; ChartMogul.

5.3 Net Promoter Score (NPS)

DefinitionCustomer loyalty measured by "How likely are you to recommend us?" (0–10). Promoters = 9–10, Detractors = 0–6.
FormulaNPS = % Promoters − % Detractors
PurposeTracks loyalty and word-of-mouth potential.
UnitScore from −100 to +100
Data RequirementsSurvey responses.
Example500 responses: 250 promoters (50%), 100 detractors (20%): NPS = 50 − 20 = +30.
SourceBain & Company, "Net Promoter System".

5.4 Customer Satisfaction Score (CSAT)

DefinitionShare of respondents satisfied with an interaction (4 or 5 on a 5-point scale).
FormulaCSAT = (Satisfied Responses ÷ Total Responses) × 100
PurposeMeasures satisfaction with a specific touchpoint (support ticket, onboarding).
UnitPercentage
Data RequirementsPost-interaction survey responses.
Example340 ÷ 400 × 100 = 85%.
SourceZendesk, "Customer Satisfaction Score".

5.5 Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)

DefinitionExpected gross profit from a customer over the whole relationship.
FormulaCLV = (ARPA × Gross Margin %) ÷ Churn Rate (per-period values)
PurposeSets how much can be spent to acquire and retain customers (CLV:CAC ≈ 3:1 is a common target).
UnitCurrency
Data RequirementsAverage revenue per account, gross margin, churn rate.
Example($100 × 0.80) ÷ 0.03 = $2,667; with CAC $200, CLV:CAC ≈ 13:1.
SourceHarvard Business School Online, "Customer Lifetime Value".
Variation: retail often uses CLV = Average Order Value × Purchase Frequency × Customer Lifespan (revenue-based, not margin-based).

6. Finance

6.1 Revenue Growth Rate

DefinitionPercentage change in revenue between two periods.
FormulaRevenue Growth = ((Current Period Revenue − Prior Period Revenue) ÷ Prior Period Revenue) × 100
PurposeMeasures the speed of business expansion.
UnitPercentage
Data RequirementsRevenue for two comparable periods (e.g. YoY).
Example(1,200,000 − 1,000,000) ÷ 1,000,000 × 100 = 20% YoY.
SourceCorporate Finance Institute (CFI), "Revenue Growth".

6.2 Gross Profit Margin

DefinitionShare of revenue left after cost of goods sold.
FormulaGross Margin = ((Revenue − COGS) ÷ Revenue) × 100
PurposeShows production/pricing efficiency.
UnitPercentage
Data RequirementsRevenue; COGS from the income statement.
Example(1,200,000 − 480,000) ÷ 1,200,000 × 100 = 60%.
SourceInvestopedia, "Gross Profit Margin"; CFI.

6.3 Net Profit Margin

DefinitionShare of revenue that remains as profit after all expenses, interest and taxes.
FormulaNet Margin = (Net Income ÷ Revenue) × 100
PurposeOverall profitability measure.
UnitPercentage
Data RequirementsNet income and revenue.
Example144,000 ÷ 1,200,000 × 100 = 12%.
SourceInvestopedia, "Net Profit Margin".

6.4 Operating Cash Flow (OCF)

DefinitionCash generated by normal business operations.
FormulaOCF = Net Income + Non-Cash Expenses (Depreciation, Amortization) ± Change in Working Capital
PurposeShows whether the business funds itself; profit is not the same as cash.
UnitCurrency
Data RequirementsIncome statement and balance-sheet changes (cash-flow statement, indirect method).
Example144,000 + 30,000 − 24,000 (receivables rose) = $150,000.
SourceIFRS Foundation, IAS 7 "Statement of Cash Flows".

6.5 Return on Investment (ROI)

DefinitionNet gain from an investment relative to its cost.
FormulaROI = ((Gain from Investment − Cost of Investment) ÷ Cost of Investment) × 100
PurposeCompares the profitability of projects or investments.
UnitPercentage
Data RequirementsTotal cost and total return attributable to the investment.
Example(65,000 − 50,000) ÷ 50,000 × 100 = 30%.
SourceInvestopedia, "Return on Investment (ROI)".

7. Operations

7.1 On-Time Delivery Rate (OTD)

DefinitionShare of orders delivered on or before the promised date.
FormulaOTD = (Orders Delivered On Time ÷ Total Orders Delivered) × 100
PurposeMeasures supply-chain reliability and customer experience.
UnitPercentage
Data RequirementsPromised and actual delivery dates.
Example1,880 ÷ 2,000 × 100 = 94%.
SourceAPQC, "Open Standards Benchmarking: Logistics".

7.2 Inventory Turnover

DefinitionHow many times inventory is sold and replaced in a period.
FormulaInventory Turnover = COGS ÷ Average Inventory Value
PurposeLow turnover signals overstock; very high turnover can signal stock-outs.
UnitTimes per period
Data RequirementsCOGS; inventory value at start and end of the period.
Example480,000 ÷ ((70,000 + 90,000) ÷ 2) = 6× per year (≈ 61 days of inventory).
SourceCFI, "Inventory Turnover Ratio".

7.3 Overall Equipment Effectiveness (OEE)

DefinitionShare of planned production time that is truly productive.
FormulaOEE = Availability × Performance × Quality
PurposeIdentifies losses from downtime, slow running and defects in manufacturing.
UnitPercentage
Data RequirementsRun time, planned time, ideal cycle time, total and good units.
Example0.90 × 0.95 × 0.98 = 83.8%.
SourceOEE.com (Vorne Industries), "OEE Calculation".

7.4 Order Cycle Time

DefinitionAverage time from receiving an order to delivering it.
FormulaOrder Cycle Time = Σ (Delivery Date − Order Date) ÷ Number of Orders
PurposeMeasures operational speed and responsiveness.
UnitDays (or hours)
Data RequirementsOrder and delivery timestamps.
Example7,000 total days ÷ 2,000 orders = 3.5 days.
SourceAPQC, Supply Chain Process Classification Framework.

8. Human Resources (HR)

8.1 Employee Turnover Rate

DefinitionShare of employees who left during a period.
FormulaTurnover = (Separations in Period ÷ Average Headcount) × 100
PurposeShows retention health; turnover is costly (hiring, training, lost productivity).
UnitPercentage
Data RequirementsSeparations; headcount at start and end.
Example24 ÷ ((190 + 210) ÷ 2) × 100 = 12% annual.
SourceSHRM, "Calculating Turnover"; U.S. BLS JOLTS methodology.

8.2 Time to Hire

DefinitionAverage days between a candidate entering the pipeline and accepting an offer.
FormulaTime to Hire = Σ (Offer Acceptance Date − Application Date) ÷ Number of Hires
PurposeMeasures recruiting efficiency and candidate experience.
UnitDays
Data RequirementsApplicant tracking system (ATS) dates.
Example840 days ÷ 30 hires = 28 days.
SourceSHRM, "Time to Fill vs Time to Hire".
Do not confuse with Time to Fill, which starts when the requisition is opened, not when the candidate applies.

8.3 Cost per Hire

DefinitionAverage cost of recruiting one new employee.
FormulaCost per Hire = (External Recruiting Costs + Internal Recruiting Costs) ÷ Total Hires
PurposeBudgets recruitment and compares sourcing channels.
UnitCurrency per hire
Data RequirementsAgency fees, ads, recruiter time, tools; hire count.
Example$120,000 ÷ 30 = $4,000.
SourceANSI/SHRM 06001.2012 "Cost-Per-Hire" standard.

8.4 Absenteeism Rate

DefinitionShare of scheduled work time lost to unplanned absence.
FormulaAbsenteeism = (Unplanned Absence Days ÷ Total Scheduled Workdays) × 100
PurposeIndicates engagement, wellbeing and productivity issues.
UnitPercentage
Data RequirementsAttendance records; scheduled days.
Example150 ÷ (200 employees × 22 days) × 100 = 3.4%.
SourceU.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, "Absences from Work".

9. Engineering

The first four KPIs below are the DORA metrics, the most widely used standard for software delivery performance.

9.1 Deployment Frequency

DefinitionHow often code is successfully deployed to production.
FormulaDeployment Frequency = Number of Production Deployments ÷ Number of Days (or Weeks) in Period
PurposeMeasures delivery throughput and batch size.
UnitDeployments per day/week
Data RequirementsCI/CD deployment logs.
Example60 deployments ÷ 30 days = 2 per day (DORA "elite").
SourceGoogle Cloud DORA, "Accelerate State of DevOps Report".

9.2 Lead Time for Changes

DefinitionTime from code commit to that code running in production.
FormulaLead Time = Median (Deploy Time − Commit Time) across changes
PurposeMeasures delivery speed.
UnitHours or days
Data RequirementsCommit timestamps linked to deployments.
ExampleMedian of change lead times = 18 hours.
SourceGoogle Cloud DORA research.

9.3 Change Failure Rate

DefinitionShare of deployments that cause a production failure requiring remediation.
FormulaChange Failure Rate = (Failed Deployments ÷ Total Deployments) × 100
PurposeMeasures delivery quality/stability.
UnitPercentage
Data RequirementsDeployments linked to incidents, rollbacks or hotfixes.
Example6 ÷ 60 × 100 = 10%.
SourceGoogle Cloud DORA research.

9.4 Mean Time to Recovery (MTTR)

DefinitionAverage time to restore service after a production failure.
FormulaMTTR = Σ (Restore Time − Incident Start Time) ÷ Number of Incidents
PurposeMeasures resilience and incident-response effectiveness.
UnitMinutes or hours
Data RequirementsIncident management timestamps.
Example300 minutes ÷ 6 incidents = 50 minutes.
SourceGoogle SRE Book; DORA research.

9.5 Defect Density

DefinitionNumber of confirmed defects relative to code size.
FormulaDefect Density = Confirmed Defects ÷ (Lines of Code ÷ 1,000)
PurposeCompares code quality across modules or releases.
UnitDefects per KLOC
Data RequirementsBug tracker; code size per module.
Example45 ÷ (90,000 ÷ 1,000) = 0.5 defects/KLOC.
SourceIEEE Std 1061 (Software Quality Metrics Methodology).

10. Analytics

10.1 Data Quality Score (Completeness)

DefinitionShare of required data fields that are populated with valid values.
FormulaCompleteness = (Valid Non-Null Required Values ÷ Total Required Values) × 100
PurposeTrustworthy analysis needs complete data; tracks pipeline health.
UnitPercentage
Data RequirementsDataset with list of required fields and validation rules.
Example98,500 ÷ 100,000 × 100 = 98.5%.
SourceDAMA International, DAMA-DMBOK (Data Quality dimensions).

10.2 Report / Dashboard Adoption Rate

DefinitionShare of intended users who actively use analytics assets.
FormulaAdoption = (Users Who Viewed Dashboards in Period ÷ Licensed or Target Users) × 100
PurposeShows whether analytics work actually supports decisions.
UnitPercentage
Data RequirementsBI tool usage logs; target user list.
Example162 ÷ 240 × 100 = 67.5%.
SourceGartner, "Data and Analytics Adoption" research; Microsoft Power BI usage metrics docs.

10.3 Forecast Accuracy (MAPE)

DefinitionAverage percentage error between forecasts and actual values.
FormulaMAPE = (1/n) × Σ |Actual − Forecast| ÷ |Actual| × 100; Forecast Accuracy = 100 − MAPE
PurposeEvaluates forecasting/model quality for planning.
UnitPercentage
Data RequirementsPaired forecast and actual values.
ExampleActuals 100, 200; forecasts 90, 220: errors 10%, 10% → MAPE = 10%, accuracy 90%.
SourceHyndman & Athanasopoulos, Forecasting: Principles and Practice, 3rd ed.

10.4 Data Pipeline Freshness (Data Latency)

DefinitionTime between an event happening and its data being available for analysis.
FormulaLatency = Time Data Is Available in Warehouse − Event Timestamp (report median and 95th percentile)
PurposeEnsures decisions use timely data; monitors SLAs.
UnitMinutes or hours
Data RequirementsEvent timestamps and load timestamps.
ExampleMedian latency 45 minutes, p95 = 2 hours against a 3-hour SLA: within target.
SourceGoogle Cloud, "Data freshness" (Dataplex data quality docs).

11. Distinguishing Similar Metrics

Metric AMetric BKey Difference
Revenue GrowthProfit MarginGrowth compares revenue across periods (speed). Margin compares profit to revenue within one period (efficiency). A company can grow fast with negative margin.
Gross MarginNet MarginGross subtracts only COGS; net subtracts all expenses, interest and taxes.
Customer ChurnRevenue Churn / NRRCustomer churn counts logos lost; NRR measures revenue and includes expansion. Losing many small customers can still give NRR > 100%.
NPSCSATNPS measures overall loyalty (recommendation); CSAT measures satisfaction with a single interaction.
ROIROASROI uses net gain (profit) over total cost; ROAS uses gross revenue over ad spend only.
Time to HireTime to FillTime to hire starts at candidate application; time to fill starts at requisition approval.
Lead Time for ChangesCycle TimeLead time is commit → production; cycle time often starts when work begins on a ticket.
Conversion RateCTRCTR is clicks per impression (top of funnel); conversion rate is completed actions per visitor (bottom of funnel).

Industry differences in calculation

  • Churn: SaaS often uses monthly churn on MRR; telecoms use monthly subscriber churn; retail, without contracts, uses "inactive for N days".
  • CLV: subscription businesses use margin ÷ churn; retail uses order value × frequency × lifespan.
  • Inventory turnover: some retailers use sales instead of COGS in the numerator, which inflates the ratio.
  • Active users: the definition of "active" (login vs a core action) must be stated, as it changes results greatly.

12. Conclusion

KPIs turn strategy into measurable targets. Used well, they let an organization:

  • Align teams on shared, unambiguous definitions, so marketing, sales and finance report the same numbers.
  • Spot problems early: rising churn, falling conversion or slower lead times signal issues before they hit revenue.
  • Allocate resources: CLV:CAC, ROAS and ROI show where each extra dollar earns the most.
  • Balance speed and quality, by pairing metrics (deployment frequency with change failure rate; growth with margin).

Good practice is to pick a small set of KPIs per objective, define numerator, denominator and period clearly, review them regularly, and remember that KPIs show correlation, not causation: a change in a KPI is a prompt to investigate, not proof of its cause.
